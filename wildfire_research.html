Wildfire Ignition Prediction in Alberta

Teaching Machines to Spot Fires Before They Start 🔥🛰️

Wildfires don’t send calendar invites. They erupt suddenly, spread aggressively, and leave devastating ecological and economic impacts behind. So I asked a simple but ambitious question: Can we predict where a wildfire is likely to ignite before it actually does?

This project is my deep-dive into wildfire ignition prediction using high-resolution geospatial data and deep learning. By combining satellite-derived land features, weather data, terrain, and historical ignition records, I built a system that doesn’t just say “fire or no fire” — it learns where an ignition is most likely to happen, pixel by pixel.

Canada experiences thousands of wildfires every year, and Alberta alone records over a thousand annually. As climate change extends fire seasons and increases extreme weather, early ignition detection has become critical for:

  • Faster emergency response and resource allocation 🚒
  • Reducing economic losses and environmental damage 🌲
  • Supporting data-driven wildfire management decisions 📡

🧠 What I Built

I designed a two-stage deep learning pipeline using U-Net architectures that work together like a security checkpoint:

  1. Stage 1 – Patch-Level Detector: A deep learning model scans 64×64 spatial patches and decides whether an ignition might exist anywhere in that region.
  2. Stage 2 – Spatial Refiner: If Stage 1 raises a flag, a second U-Net zooms in to produce a high-resolution ignition probability map at 25 m resolution.

This cascade design dramatically reduces false alarms while preserving extremely high recall — a critical requirement in wildfire prediction, where missing an ignition is far more costly than raising a false alert.

📊 Key Results

  • ~94.5% patch-level recall — almost every ignition region is detected
  • ~94% pixel-level recall — ignition shapes are spatially well-captured
  • Extremely low pixel false-positive rate (~0.046%)
  • Stable performance on fully unseen test years (2021–2023)

These results demonstrate that a carefully designed two-stage architecture can handle the extreme class imbalance inherent in wildfire ignition prediction while remaining operationally practical.

🚀 What’s Next

This project is designed to scale. Future extensions include:

  • Incorporating multi-day weather sequences (temporal modeling)
  • Cause-specific ignition models (human vs lightning)
  • Real-time inference via APIs and dashboards
  • Cloud-native deployment with GPU acceleration

The ultimate goal? A real-time wildfire intelligence system that supports early warning, smarter resource deployment, and better protection of communities and ecosystems.
