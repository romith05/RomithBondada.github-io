Wildfire Risk Dashboard & Prevention Insights (Excel)

Turning wildfire records into decision-ready dashboards — with Excel doing the heavy lifting 🔥📊

Wildfires aren’t just random events — they follow patterns. The challenge is spotting those patterns fast enough to turn data into decisions. So I built an Excel-based analytics dashboard that transforms historical wildfire records into clear insights about when fires occur, what causes them, what conditions make them worse, and where prevention efforts should focus.

The Mission: From Data Mess → Risk Story 🧭

My goal was to create an executive-ready dashboard suite starting in Excel, focused on real-world wildfire risk. Instead of just making “pretty charts,” I built a workflow that answers the kind of questions decision-makers actually ask: Which months are the riskiest? What drives severity? Where should prevention money go?

This project uses a large historical wildfire dataset (24k+ records) and applies:

  1. Data cleaning + preparation (Power Query, standardizing categories, handling missing values)
  2. Risk scoring to rank months and conditions by impact
  3. Cause → Weather → Outcome modeling to explain severity (not just count fires)
  4. Prevention segmentation to recommend targeted strategies

✨ Objectives

  1. Build an executive dashboard summarizing wildfire activity, trends, and risk over time.
  2. Quantify risk seasonality (Top Risk Months) using a data-driven scoring approach.
  3. Create a Cause → Weather → Outcome framework to explain when and why fires become severe.
  4. Translate insights into prevention and readiness recommendations (“Where should prevention money go?”).

🧐 Key Insights

  • Human-caused ignitions dominate overall fire counts, but severity depends heavily on conditions.
  • High-wind conditions amplify wildfire outcomes, especially for natural ignitions — a key trigger for readiness.
  • Risk is seasonal: late spring / early summer tends to dominate the risk profile, making prevention timing critical.
  • Large fires drive total burned area even when they occur less frequently — frequency ≠ impact.

🧪 What I Built (Dashboard Suite)

  • Wildfire Situation Overview — KPIs, yearly trends, monthly pattern, and a ranked “Top Risk Months” table.
  • Cause & Prevention Insights — Pareto of causes, size-class impact over time, fuel-type severity/spread, resource deployment view.
  • Cause → Weather → Outcome Analysis — percentile-based weather categories and matrices that explain severity conditions.
  • Prevention Decision Table — segmenting risk into actionable strategies (education/patrols, seasonal restrictions, early detection & pre-positioning).

🧠 The “Cause → Weather → Outcome” Framework

Instead of stopping at “fires by cause,” I connected three layers: Ignition Cause → Weather Condition (percentile bins) → Outcome Severity. This made it possible to explain why certain events become large and where interventions deliver the highest impact per dollar.

💡 Where Should Prevention Money Go?

Based on the dashboard evidence, prevention should prioritize:

  1. Human-caused prevention during peak risk months (education, patrols, seasonal restrictions).
  2. Condition-triggered readiness for high-wind days (early detection and pre-positioning crews).
  3. Targeted approaches by segment — not one-size-fits-all spending across the year.
