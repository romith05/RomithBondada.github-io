🏙 Calgary Citizen Satisfaction Survey Analysis

“Because knowing what makes people happy (or not) in Calgary isn’t just polite—it’s data science.”

In this project, I dove into Calgary’s 2021 Citizen Satisfaction Survey, which asked thousands of residents how they feel about life in the city—from parks and potholes to transit and taxes. Our goal? To uncover what really drives satisfaction in Calgary. Is it clean streets? Snow removal? Education? Or maybe just fewer potholes? Using real survey data, we ran deep statistical analyses to figure out who’s happy, who’s not, and—more importantly—why.

🧰 Tools Used
  1. R (for everything from data wrangling to modeling)
  2. RMarkdown and ggplot2 (for sleek visualizations)
  3. Logistic Regression, Decision Trees, Chi-Square Tests
  4. Stratified Sampling and Upsampling (to give minority classes some love 💙)

📈 What We Did

  1. Wrangled over 2,500 responses from Calgary’s open data portal, selecting key demographic and satisfaction variables.
  2. Transformed satisfaction scores into a clean binary classification—"Yes" (satisfied) vs "No" (not so much).
  3. Visualized trends in satisfaction across income levels, age groups, education, and even Calgary’s quadrants (spoiler: NW residents are the happiest 🤠).
  4. Ran Logistic Regression and Classification Tree models to predict satisfaction—and wrestled with class imbalance using upsampling and threshold tuning.
  5. Evaluated models with accuracy, precision, recall, and cross-validation to keep things honest.
  6. Discovered that services like 911, parks, and snow removal had the biggest impact on overall satisfaction.

🧐 Key Insights

  • Younger residents tend to be happier. (Maybe because they don’t shovel snow?)
  • Income matters—more money, more satisfaction. Who knew? 😉
  • Long-term residents are a bit grumpier. (Maybe those potholes add up over time.)
  • Not all services are created equal—some like flood protection and community support were statistically significant drivers of happiness.

🌟 Why It Matters

This wasn’t just about crunching numbers—it was about helping the City of Calgary make smarter, citizen-focused decisions. The insights from this project can guide better service planning, improve resident engagement, and maybe even make life in Calgary a bit brighter for everyone.

🧠 Final Thoughts

While data can reveal trends and highlight what usually makes people satisfied, it can't fully capture the beautiful chaos of human emotion. After all, life satisfaction isn’t just about income, services, or even snow removal—sometimes it’s about how your day started, who smiled at you on the train, or whether your coffee spilled. So no, I can't predict happiness with 100% accuracy—because feelings are fluid, messy, and delightfully unpredictable. But with the right data and a bit of empathy, we can get close enough to make cities a little more human.
