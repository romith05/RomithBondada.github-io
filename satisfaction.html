Calgary Citizen Satisfaction Survey Analysis

My Quest to Uncover Calgary's Happiest (and Not-So-Happiest) Citizens! 📊🤠

Ever wonder what makes a city tick? Or, more accurately, what makes its citizens say, "Yep, I'm pretty darn satisfied with this place!"? Well, wonder no more! I donned my detective hat and dove headfirst into the fascinating world of citizen satisfaction. And let me tell you, it was a wild ride of numbers, insights, and a surprising amount of satisfaction (pun intended!).

The Grand Unveiling: What We Explored 🕵️‍♀️

The City of Calgary, bless their data-loving hearts, conducts a biennial Citizen Satisfaction Survey. My mission, should I choose to accept it (and I totally did!), was to dissect their 2018–2021 dataset. My goal? To figure out what makes Calgarians tick when it comes to their city services and overall quality of life.

We're talking about unearthing how things like:
  1. Income (because, let's be real, money can buy a little happiness... or at least a nicer house)💰
  2. Homeownership Status (renting vs. owning – a tale as old as time, or at least as old as property taxes!) 🏡
  3. Age and Education (because wisdom and degrees might just give you a different perspective) 👵👨‍🎓
  4. Perceptions of City Services (from those oh-so-smooth roads to the ever-reliable public transit) 🚌🛣️

✨ Objectives

  1. Examine the distribution of overall satisfaction scores (q11a) among respondents.
  2. Identify the strongest statistical predictors of quality of life among the demographic variables
  3. Explore underlying clusters in the population based on service perception and demographics.

🧐 Key Insights

  • The overall satisfaction scores among Calgary citizens are decidedly optimistic, with a clear skew towards the higher end of the scale.
  • The coefficient bar chart identified Age_Group (negative impact) and Education Level (positive impact) as the strongest overall statistical predictors of satisfaction among demographic variables.
  • Clustering with GMM revealed natural groupings within the citizen population, moving beyond simple satisfaction scores to uncover underlying patterns.
